> Isn't contact part of a foul call? Infraction is infraction right?

**Redrafted:** 2026-09-04 (daily-reel-render)

Both halves of the note are correct, and v1's framing is what invited them.

- **"Isn't contact part of a foul call?"** Yes — contact is the *definition* of
  a foul. 15.1: "A breach of the rules due to non-minor contact between two or more opposing players is a foul." So the marker's bump is a foul from the moment it
  happens.
- **"Infraction is infraction right?"** Also yes. 15.2: "A breach of the rules regarding a Marking or Travel breach is an infraction. Infractions do not stop play." An
  infraction is defined by the kind of breach it is, not by who calls it.

v1 said "the same contact gives you two calls", which reads as though "Foul" and
"Contact" name two different events. They don't. 17.6.1.3 does not reclassify
the breach; it offers the thrower a choice of **remedy** — "the thrower may
choose to call a contact infraction" — for a Defensive Throwing Foul that its
own opening clause says has already occurred. The reel now says that out loud
instead of sliding past it.

What changed:

- **A third topic/rules pair, carding 15.1 and 15.2.** Scene 4 is the new
  `#2   STILL A FOUL, DIFFERENT CALL`, headline `"Contact" is not a different
  event.`, answering the note directly with the two definitions reel 21 already
  taught. Seven scenes to nine, the standard three-pair shape.
- **The hook.** "You can stop the game over it — or you can make the call that
  keeps you playing" → "That is a foul — and you still don't have to stop the
  game over it." The concession comes first now, so the reel never implies the
  contact was anything other than a foul.
- **The old `#2` becomes `#3`, and its kicker changes.** `THE CALL THAT KEEPS
  PLAY LIVE` → `YOU CHOOSE IF PLAY STOPS`. Incidentally retires the tightest
  kicker in the run: the old one measured 896 of the 900px column, four pixels
  of headroom. The new one is 772.
- **Both captions** gained the "if contact is what makes it a foul, how can it
  be an infraction?" section, quoting 15.1 and 15.2 in full, plus a closing line
  on the one limit — the choice only exists for contact before the release and
  not during the throwing motion. Block-quoted rule text elsewhere is untouched.
- **The example beat** now names the remedy explicitly: "What it changes is the
  remedy", then the two calls.

What did **not** change: the lesson number, the topic, the four rules v1 already
cited, the field tip, and the hashtag and attribution blocks. 18.1.3's
"minus one (1)" restart stays off every card — a contact infraction has its own
remedy under 17.6.1.3 and resumes at one, which is a further reason not to
describe it as simply "a marking infraction".
