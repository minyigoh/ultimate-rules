## Instagram caption

"I was going for the disc."

It's the most-used sentence on any pitch, and the rulebook takes the trouble to name it and rule it out.

12.6 — all players must attempt to avoid initiating contact, and there is no situation where a player may justify initiating contact. Then 12.6.1 closes the door on the specific excuse: "making a play for the disc" is not a valid one.

That isn't the same as saying every bump is a foul. 12.8 allows minor contact when two players move toward the same point at the same time. The line isn't contact. The line is initiating it.

And if you're still arguing about who did, 12.7 gives you three tests: who arrived after the other player already held the position, who changed their line and made contact unavoidable, and — if nobody can agree — who left their feet.

Save this one for the next sideline debate.

## 30-second script (if you want the Reel version)

**HOOK (0:00–0:04)**
"I was going for the disc" is not a defence. It's in the rulebook that it isn't.

**EXPLANATION (0:04–0:16)**
Rule 12.6: every player has to attempt to avoid initiating contact, and there's no situation where you can justify initiating it. 12.6.1 names the excuse directly — making a play for the disc doesn't cover you.

**EXAMPLE (0:16–0:26)**
Doesn't mean every bump is a foul. 12.8 says minor contact happens when two people move to the same point. But if you arrived after they'd already taken that spot, or you changed your line into them — 12.7 says that one's on you.

**CTA (0:26–0:30)**
One rule a day. Follow along.
