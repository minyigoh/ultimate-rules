### Why the title is "the fine print"

This is a week of detail rules. Not one of the seven is a headline rule a
beginner meets in their first game: three ways to turn it over without being
touched, the no-boosting rule, a turnover nobody noticed, the legality of
claiming space, a contested goal, the fine print on a contested stall-out, and
the delay-of-game remedy. Each one is the small print behind something simpler
the account has already taught.

**The title was checked against the last slide, not just the theme of the first
five**, which is the carousel-post-6 v1 defect. Slide 8 is lesson 70, delay of
game — a procedural remedy almost nobody knows is in the rulebook, which is as
squarely "fine print" as anything on the deck. Slide 5 (lesson 67, claiming
space) was the one to check hardest, because positioning feels like technique
rather than small print; it is on the deck as the *legality* of positioning,
rules 12.4/12.5/12.9, which is fine print about where you may run.

The phrase is also the block's own language: lesson 69 is titled "The fine print
on stall-outs", so the cover is taking a word the week already used rather than
imposing a theme on it.

## Instagram caption

Week ten, all in one place. Seven lessons of small print — the ways you lose the disc without being beaten, where you may stand, what counts as a goal, and what the clock lets you get away with.

Three ways to turn it over by yourself. The three ways you hand it over without a defender touching it. A wobbly throw that comes back to you in the wind is fine if a defender touched it. If nobody did, it's yours and you've lost it.

No boosting, no props. Why you cannot be lifted, launched or propped to reach a disc. This includes well-meant helping hands. If you'd lift a team-mate to reach one, don't.

When play carried on after a turnover nobody noticed. What happens when the turnover was real but nobody saw it. If you think it hit the ground, say so straight away. Waiting to see what happens next makes it much harder to resolve.

Claiming space legally. Where you are allowed to run when you are both going for it. Under a floaty disc, move to where the disc is going, not to where your opponent wants to run.

Contested goals. Who may call a goal, and the moment it is deemed to have been scored. If it's close, look down at your feet immediately rather than celebrating. You'll be asked, and you should know.

The fine print on stall-outs. What a contested stall-out actually costs you. A contested stall-out restarts the count at eight, not one. Contesting buys you very little time.

Delay of game. The remedy for a team that will not get on with it. Teams must also prepare for the pull without unreasonable delay. Huddles between points have a natural limit.

Each slide carries its rule numbers, so you can check any of it against the rulebook rather than taking our word for it.

That's seventy of seventy-five.
