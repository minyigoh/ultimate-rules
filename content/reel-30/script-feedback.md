**Redrafted:** 2026-09-04 (daily-reel-render)

The note is right, and v1 was wrong on the rule rather than merely blunt about
it. 12.9 reads: "Players may not use their extended arms or legs to obstruct the movement of opposing players." — *extended* arms or legs, used to obstruct. Its
annotation narrows it further: a player's arms and legs are not considered
"extended" during normal running and jumping. Nothing in it touches where you
put your body. And 12.5, which this reel cards two scenes earlier, grants the
position outright: "Every player is entitled to occupy any position on the field not occupied by any opposing player, provided that they do not initiate contact in taking such a position, and are not moving in a reckless or dangerously aggressive manner."

So holding a spot with your body is legal, subject to 12.5's two conditions.
v1's scene 6 headline — "You cannot box out." — asserted the opposite, on a
slide whose own body copy ("Position is where your body is, not how far you can
reach") said the right thing. The headline is the line that reads, and it was
broader than any rule on screen.

What changed — copy only, no rules card, rule number or rule text moved:

- **Scene 6 headline.** "You cannot box out." → "You can box out — just not
  with your arms." The kicker `ARMS AND LEGS ARE NOT POSITION` was already
  correct and is untouched; it still auto-fits to 32px at 895 of the 900px
  column.
- **Scene 6 body**, rewritten to name the permission before the limit:
  standing your ground between an opponent and the disc is legal, 12.5 already
  said the space is yours, and what 12.9 bans is extended arms or legs used to
  obstruct.
- **Scene 8, the field tip.** Headline "Boxing out is a habit from another
  sport." → "Box out with your body, not your arms." The old headline framed the
  whole manoeuvre as foreign to ultimate; the new one keeps the useful half of
  the advice, which v1's body already had ("take the position with your feet").
- **The Example beat** now says the body position is fine before it says the
  arm is not.
- **Both captions.** The Instagram limb paragraph gained "Read that narrowly,
  because it is narrow. You *can* box out…"; the TikTok caption's closing line
  "boxing out is a basketball habit. here it's just illegal" is gone and
  replaced with the correct distinction. Block-quoted rule text in both is
  untouched.

What did **not** change: the lesson number, the topic, the three rules cited
(12.5, 17.4.1, 12.9), their order, the nine-scene shape, scenes 1–5, 7 and 9.
